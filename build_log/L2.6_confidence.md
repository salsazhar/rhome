**Layer:** L2 — Inference engine
**Date:** 23 August 2026
**Status:** ✅ Passed
**Tests:** 698 total (15 added this stage, plus 82 from extending Guardrail 3)

---

## Purpose

Report, per channel, how much evidence stands behind each displayed number — and say which component is holding it down.

Confidence is not a probability and `RESEARCH.md` §17 forbids presenting it as one. It does not say how likely an estimate is to be correct. It says how much evidence there is, which is a different and more honest claim.

## Why per channel

A single global scalar would be actively misleading, because the conditions that undermine one channel leave the others alone. A fortnight away from the gym makes fatigue states **more** certain — they have decayed to near zero and there is nothing left to be uncertain about — while making capacity estimates stale.

So $C_F$ carries no training-recency term at all. That is `DATA.md` §19.5's deliberately counter-intuitive criterion, and it is the one this stage exists to get right: an absence of training is *informative* about fatigue, not a gap in the evidence. The test drives fourteen empty days through the engine and requires $C_F$ unchanged to nine decimal places while capacity confidence falls.

## Behaviour over the development window

520 days: **499 high, 18 moderate, 3 limited**. After day 60, $C_F$ sits between 0.849 and 1.000, median 0.987.

**What binds, and when:**

| Period | Binding reason | Days |
|---|---|---:|
| First 42 days | baseline maturity | 41 of 42 |
| After day 60 | dose quality | 347 |
| | wearable completeness | 93 |
| | unexplained autonomic deviation | 20 |

The early period is exactly what §19.5 asks for: confidence rises monotonically as the 42-day baselines fill, and maturity is what holds it down throughout. Afterwards the binding reason tracks real events — nights the ring was not worn, and the stretches where autonomic readings ran further from expectation than the conditional model predicts.

### Fit quality found the simulator's noise structure

Capacity confidence combines observation density with fit quality. The five worst-fitting exercises are:

| Exercise | Fit confidence | Compoundness |
|---|---:|---:|
| triceps_pressdown | 0.756 | 0.20 |
| lateral_raise | 0.767 | 0.22 |
| hanging_leg_raise | 0.768 | 0.30 |
| reverse_pec_deck | 0.848 | 0.20 |
| lat_pulldown | 0.893 | 0.50 |

Bench press, incline dumbbell press and overhead press all sit at 1.000. Correlation between compoundness and fit confidence is +0.363.

The inverse-crime audit records that the simulator gives less compound movements **larger** performance noise — scale 0.029 to 0.050 — and that the engine assumes constant variance and is never told otherwise. Fit quality recovered that structure from residuals alone. It is a small result, but it is the kind that matters: a confidence index that reports uniform confidence regardless of what is happening is decoration.

## Findings

### Guardrail 3 was checking a third of the parameters

The registry test verified `CORE_PARAMETERS` — 13 entries — and had never looked at `ENGINE_PARAMETERS`, which by this stage held 86. Every parameter added since L2.1 was unverified against the documents.

This is the second time this guardrail has been found decorative. At L0.2 it searched whole documents and passed on a coincidental match; now it was scoped correctly but pointed at one of two registries. Both times it reported green.

Extended to all 99 parameters, four failed:

- `lambda_systemic = 0.03`, written `0.030` in the document to line up with its neighbours. The matcher now accepts trailing zeros as typography.
- `capacity_initial_observations = 3` and `capacity_confident_observations = 8`, written in `MODEL.tex` as "the first three" and "at least eight". The document now states the numerals, because these are parameter values and the specification should be precise about them.
- `body_mass_window_days = 7`, written as "a centred seven-day median". Same fix.

No parameter had actually drifted from its documented value. That is a relief rather than a vindication — nothing was checking.

### Warm-up sets were suppressing dose confidence

Dose quality weights each set by its surviving contribution $\sigma_i$. Warm-ups carry no dose, so $\sigma_i = 0$, and a first pass that fell back to a default weight of one put 1 848 unrated warm-ups into the average at the lowest quality grade.

Dose confidence sat at **0.688 for the entire record** and bound almost every day. With the section's own weighting applied — a zero contribution is a zero weight — it reads 0.987.

The tell was that the number never moved. A component that reports the same value on every day of a two-year record is not measuring anything about that record.

### Renormalising the accumulators restored decayed evidence

Dose quality is a ratio of two sums that decay together, so it is carried as two accumulators rather than a list of every set ever performed. To keep them away from underflow after a long layoff, the first version rescaled both to make the denominator one whenever it got small.

That preserves the ratio at the instant it happens and destroys everything afterwards. The magnitude of those sums is not free: it is precisely what decides how much an old set counts against a new one. Rescaling to one gave a six-week-old set full weight against a set performed today — a test that expected a fresh high-quality set to dominate got 0.80 instead of 1.00.

Now only a floor is applied, with both sums scaled by the same constant to reach it. The floor sits at $10^{-250}$, which is reached after roughly four years without training, and a set that old counting for nothing is the correct answer.

### A robust spread, not a standard deviation

`residual_spread` used a plain standard deviation. The section specifies a robust one, and the difference is not cosmetic: one genuinely bad session — an illness, a missed meal — would read as a model that is not tracking the exercise, which is the opposite of what fit confidence is for. It now uses the median absolute deviation scaled to agree with the standard deviation on normal data. A test plants a single −0.45 residual among nineteen small ones and requires fit confidence to stay above 0.9.

---

## Decisions

**A set with no effort rating takes the low intensity weight.** It is weaker evidence than a set whose rating merely sat outside the tabulated domain, but the section defines no fourth grade. Taking the most cautious value available is preferable to inventing a parameter here.

**Confidence is computed on rest days too.** Its inputs — wearable completeness, baseline maturity, staleness — all move on a day with no training. A confidence figure that only exists on training days would be stale exactly when the athlete is most likely to be asking.

**Ties in the binding reason break toward the earlier component.** So the displayed label is stable from day to day rather than flickering between two equal causes.

## Corrected at L3.6

Capacity confidence in this stage combined observation density with fit
quality, and both were satisfied long before the engine was worth trusting on
an exercise. The Checkpoint 2 ablation measured the gap: through an exercise's
first ten sessions, 88.5% of readings said `high` while the engine was losing
to a median-of-the-last-three baseline.

The cause was a definitional slip. Observation density counts eligible sets in
the last 28 days — a *recency* check — and nothing was asking whether the
engine had ever seen enough of the exercise to have an opinion. L3.6 adds a
capacity-maturity component counting lifetime sessions, and the first ten
sessions of an exercise now contain **no** `high` readings at all.

## Notes carried forward

- $C_F$, $C_N$ and $C_G$ are the same value, as the section defines them. They are reported as three fields anyway, because L2.8 projects them separately and the display distinguishes them.
- The library is entirely curated, so the derived-coefficient penalty is exercised on a constructed exercise. L5.1 makes that case common and it should be re-measured then.
- Fit confidence bottoms out at 0.756 on the synthetic data. Real logs will scatter more; whether the 0.40 floor is reached is a question for L3.9.
