## The change: capacity maturity

**The defect, measured at Checkpoint 2.** Through an exercise's first ten sessions, **88.5% of capacity-confidence readings said `high`** while the engine's performance prediction was losing to a median-of-the-last-three baseline. Mean confidence in that period was 0.910 — no lower than the 0.928 it reports once settled.

**The cause was a definitional slip, not a bug.** Capacity confidence combined observation density with fit quality. Density counts eligible sets in the last 28 days, which is a statement about *recency*: it asks whether you have trained this lift lately. Nothing was asking whether the engine had ever seen enough of it to have an opinion. Those come apart — an athlete returning to a lift after a year satisfies the density term within a fortnight, on an estimate resting on four observations.

`MODEL.tex` gains a §Capacity maturity, and $C_{P,e}$ becomes $C_{\mathrm{obs},e}\cdot C_{\mathrm{fit},e}\cdot C_{\mathrm{mat},e}$ with

$$C_{\mathrm{mat},e} = 0.45 + 0.55\min\left(1, \frac{m_e}{25}\right)$$

over lifetime sessions $m_e$. The saturation is measured rather than chosen: the ablation puts the crossing near 20 and the clear advantage at 30.

### The result

| Sessions of that exercise | n | Mean confidence | Bands |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1–10 | 270 | **0.551** | 202 moderate, 68 limited, **0 high** |
| 11–20 | 270 | 0.726 | 140 high, 107 moderate, 23 limited |
| 21–30 | 270 | 0.852 | 212 high, 46 moderate, 12 limited |
| 31+ | 1 273 | 0.928 | 1 178 high, 89 moderate, 6 limited |

Before: 238 of the first 270 read `high`. After: **none**. Confidence now rises to `high` at roughly the point the engine starts beating the baseline, which is what the index is for.

### In plain terms

Say you add a new exercise. For the first ten times you do it, the model doesn't really know what to expect from you — and until now it said "high confidence" anyway, because you'd been doing it *recently*. Recently and enough are different things. It now says "moderate" or "limited" for those first sessions, and tells you why: *"6 of 25 sessions on this exercise all-time"*. Once you've done it twenty-odd times it earns its confidence back.

## Two errors caught while building it

**Counting sets where the ablation counted sessions.** The first version incremented on every eligible set. Three sets of bench in one session is one session's worth of evidence about bench, not three, and counting sets saturated the term after about nine sessions — inside the very period it exists to flag. Confidence in the first ten sessions read 0.757 with 141 still `high`.

**A saturated component reporting itself as the binding reason.** Ties in the binding reason break toward the earlier component, so placing maturity first made it win every tie — and on settled exercises, where everything sits at 1.0 and *nothing* is binding, the app would have said "limited by: capacity maturity — enough history on this exercise". True, and the opposite of informative. Ordering in that tuple is not cosmetic.
