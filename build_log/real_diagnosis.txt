2. HOW MUCH WORK IS SUPPRESSION DOING?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The engine predicts capacity x (1 - suppression). 'Last observation'
predicts the previous result and applies no suppression at all. If the
athlete's session-to-session variation is smaller than the suppression
the engine applies, then suppression is adding error, not removing it.

  suppression at projection time (n=292)
    median 2.40%   p90 3.82%   max 4.75%

  session-to-session change in observed performance (n=231)
    mean +0.0152   mean |change| 0.1207   sd 0.2124

  mean suppression applied     0.0235
  mean absolute real movement  0.1207

3. IS CAPACITY LAGGING, AND IS THE HUBER CLIP WHY?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The clip is k times each exercise-context's robust session scale. If an
innovation exceeds it on most updates, capacity is rate-limited and
cannot track a moving target however wrong it is.

  updates where the clip binds : 119/271 (43.9%)
  median |innovation|          : 0.1302   (median clip 0.1345)
  mean innovation              : +0.1541

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WITHIN A SESSION, DOES THE FATIGUE CORRECTION HOLD?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Capacity learns from every eligible set, each corrected by dividing out
the fatigue acting at that moment. If the correction is right, the first
and last set of a session should imply the same capacity. If the last
still implies less, later sets drag capacity below what the scored set
(always the first) supports, and the next projection comes in low.

  sessions with >=2 scored sets : 301
  last vs first, raw            : -0.0962 median (-9.17%)
  last vs first, fatigue-corrected: -0.0442 median (-4.32%)

  If the corrected figure is still materially negative, the engine's
  suppression does not account for what happens inside a session.

5. WHAT IS THE ATHLETE ACTUALLY LOGGING?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Nuzzo inversion turns effort into relative intensity. If the logged
effort is not what the curve assumes, every capacity estimate inherits
the error, and no amount of pooling or clipping fixes it.
