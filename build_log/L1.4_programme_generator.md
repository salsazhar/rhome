## Purpose

Decide what Athlete A sets out to do: which days they train, which exercises, how many sets, at what repetitions and how close to failure.

This module produces *intentions*. What the athlete achieves depends on realised performance, which is L1.5's concern. The separation matters: a programme that always went to plan would remove exactly the variation the engine needs to model.

## Findings

### Hand-tuning a five-constraint problem oscillates

Satisfying the rep bands broke parity; fixing parity broke per-muscle volume; fixing that broke the rep bands again. Four rounds of manual adjustment made no net progress.

Replaced with a hill-climber over the template set counts, scoring all constraints at once. That found a solution in seconds — but it was **not a programme any coach would write**: six sets of calf raises, five of hip adduction, one set of cable rows. Numerically optimal, and training nonsense.

Adding domain bounds (compounds 2–5 sets, isolation 2–4) produced a solution that satisfies the metrics *and* reads as real training. The lesson is worth keeping: an optimiser will meet the objective by distorting anything the objective does not constrain, so the plausibility constraints have to be stated rather than assumed. Recorded as **D11**.

### Unrecovered missed sessions were the sole cause of parity violations

Parity sat stubbornly outside ±10% on the worst blocks despite several attempted fixes — region-aware missing, rotating light weeks, tuned probabilities. Rather than guess again, I ablated each stochastic element in turn:

| Configuration | Median | p95 | Max | Within ±10% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | 2.8% | 11.0% | 17.9% | 93.4% |
| No substitutions | 3.2% | 10.9% | 14.6% | 93.9% |
| **No misses** | 2.8% | 5.1% | 6.9% | **100%** |
| Neither | 3.2% | 3.2% | 3.2% | 100% |

Unambiguous: substitutions were irrelevant, misses were everything. Several hours of tuning had been aimed at the wrong mechanism, and one ablation table settled it.

The fix follows the specification's own structure. `DATA.md` §3.2 exempts labelled scenarios from the parity requirement, so genuine absence belongs to S6 (travel) and S12 (missed-session block) rather than to background noise. Ordinary life shifts a session within the week instead of removing it — which is also what athletes actually do. Rest days sit at template slots 2 and 6, so every make-up lands in the same week as the session it replaces.

### A one-slot queue silently lost the second miss of a week

After make-ups became certain, 2.1% of blocks still failed. The cause was a single `pending` slot: two misses before a rest day meant one was dropped. Making it a two-item queue — the week has two rest days — took compliance from 97.9% to 99.5%.

### A correction that ran backwards when it was needed least

`_slot_to_omit` chose the light week's omitted session by asking which leaned hardest toward the region that was ahead. When the athlete was *already balanced*, that logic picked the most lopsided session available and created the imbalance it existed to prevent.

Rewritten to minimise the imbalance *remaining* after the omission, which correctly selects the most region-neutral session when balance is already good.

---

## Decisions

**Templates were tuned by measurement, not by judgement.** The constraints interact too strongly for intuition. The hill-climber is kept in `/tmp` rather than the repository because it is a one-off tool; the tuned templates and the tests that pin them are the durable artefacts.

**Genuine absence lives only in labelled scenarios.** Ordinary weeks reschedule rather than skip. This keeps unexplained absence out of the background, where it would break a documented invariant, and puts it where the evaluation can reason about it.

**Light weeks are planned, not random.** `DATA.md` §18 states five sessions a week is an average. A planned omission whose session is chosen to preserve parity is region-neutral by construction; a random one is not.

---

## Deviations from specification

None. Parity is met at 99.5% of blocks with a median of 2.5%, which is a fair reading of the specification's "approximately ±10%". The three exceeding blocks reach 12.7% at worst, and a test pins both the 95% floor and a 20% ceiling so the tail cannot silently widen.

## Notes carried forward

- L1.5 consumes `SessionPlan` objects and realises them against the athlete's performance. Achieved repetitions will differ from prescribed ranges; the §9.1 distribution should be re-measured on *realised* sets at that point, not assumed to carry over.
- `ProgrammeGenerator` holds no progression state yet. Load selection and double progression belong to L1.5, where realised performance is known.
- The scenario engine (L1.6) drives blocks through `volume_multiplier` and `rir_offset`, and true absence through S6 and S12.
