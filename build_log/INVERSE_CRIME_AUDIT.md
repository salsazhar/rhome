# Inverse-crime audit

**Regenerated at:** L1.3 · **Date:** 22 August 2026 · **Seed:** 20260822

Generated by `tools/inverse_crime_audit.py`. Every number below is measured from the running code, not asserted by hand.

## Why this document exists

Rhome is developed against synthetic data, which creates a specific risk. If the simulator that generates the data and the engine that analyses it were the same equations run in opposite directions, the engine would succeed at inverting its own generator. Every evaluation result would look excellent and none would mean anything.

`DATA.md` §16.2 requires the simulator to be structurally richer than the engine. This audit records where the two differ and by how much.

The standard is not that the two disagree — noise would achieve that. It is that the simulator contains **structure the engine's functional form cannot represent**, so no amount of parameter fitting recovers it exactly.

## 1. Local fatigue: two components versus one

The engine models local fatigue as a single exponential with one half-life per muscle. The simulator splits every impulse between a fast metabolic component (~7 h) and a slow component at the athlete's own sampled half-life, with damaging work loading the slow component preferentially.

Fitting the best single exponential to the simulator's 96-hour recovery curve leaves a maximum relative error of **6.6%** and a mean of **3.9%**. The implied single half-life is 31.9 h, against a true slow-component half-life of 31.8 h.

The misfit is systematic rather than random: the engine over-predicts early recovery and under-predicts the tail, because it is fitting one curve to a sum of two. That sign pattern is the signature of the misspecification, and it is what makes the error a bias rather than noise.

The size of the misfit depends on the session, because the split between fast and slow components does. Work taken close to failure loads the slow component preferentially, which makes the total decay *more* like a single exponential; moderate work leaves the two components more balanced and the single-exponential approximation correspondingly worse.

| Session | Worst single-exponential error |
|---|---:|
| 5 × 5 to failure (0 RIR) | 6.6% |
| 4 × 5 at 1 RIR | 10.1% |
| 4 × 8 at 2 RIR | 13.8% |
| 3 × 12 at 3 RIR | 18.1% |

So the engine is least wrong precisely where training is hardest, and most wrong on ordinary moderate work — which is the bulk of the dataset.

## 2. State-dependent accumulation

Engine impulses are independent of current state: ten sets deliver exactly ten times one set. In the simulator, a set landing on already-fatigued tissue does more.

The engine, being linear in set count, cannot produce this curvature at any parameter setting.

## 3. Volume interacting with proximity to failure

Required explicitly by `DATA.md` §16.2. The engine multiplies its effort term φ_F(q) and repetition term ψ_R(r) independently, so in it volume and failure **cannot** interact. In the simulator the failure penalty grows with near-failure work already performed in the session.

## 5. Functional forms

Where the engine interpolates lookup tables, the simulator uses continuous functions with different curvature. They agree near the middle of the range and diverge at its edges — so a model calibrated on typical sets will mis-handle the extremes.

The engine's repetition term rises linearly and clips; the simulator's saturates. They cross in the 8–12 range, which is where most training sits, so the divergence shows up in low-repetition strength work and high-repetition accessory work rather than in the bulk of the data.

Neuromuscular strain differs in exponent as well as form: the engine uses κ·u², the simulator κ^1.2·u^2.4.

## 6. Athlete-level parameters the engine never receives

- **Sixteen distinct local half-lives**, 24.4–37.8 h. The engine assumes one value of 30 h for all.
- **Systemic half-life 31.6 h** against an engine prior of 30 h; **neuromuscular 23.8 h** against a prior of 24 h.
- **Per-exercise RIR bias**, -0.64 to +0.91 repetitions. The engine assumes reported RIR is unbiased.
- **Heteroskedastic performance noise**, scale 0.029–0.050, larger on less compound movements. The engine assumes constant variance.

## 7. Recovery context: measurement, not readout

The engine derives its recovery multiplier from five wearable channels standardised against 42-day personal baselines. The simulator generates those channels as noisy *measurements* of a hidden daily recovery state driven by training load, sleep, an unobserved stress process and chance.

**The latent stress process.** A day-level stress variable perturbs sleep, HRV and resting heart rate together, persisting across days with an autocorrelation near 0.8. The engine sees three channels move in concert with no visible cause and no field reporting it. It is the single largest source of recovery-context error the engine cannot attribute.

**HRV is not a load readout.** This is the property that decides whether the engine's caution about single readings is justified or merely timid.

- A high-load day shifts the log-RMSSD distribution by **0.32 standard deviations** — real, and learnable across dozens of observations.
- But **40% of high-load days still carry an above-baseline reading**. An engine treating one low night as proof of under-recovery is wrong roughly two times in five.

**Deload rebound appears in 76% of runs**, not all of them. Scenario S3 expects autonomic rebound after a deload, and an engine that requires it every time will be wrong about one run in five.

**Recovery truth is wider than the engine can express.** The engine clips its multiplier to [0.80, 1.15]. True recovery propensity spans [0.68, 1.28], so at the extremes the engine cannot represent the athlete's actual recovery quality however well it is calibrated.

**Measurement error sits on top of everything**, at magnitudes taken from `RESEARCH.md` §13.3: RMSSD 6% multiplicative, resting HR 0.7 bpm, total sleep 12 min, body mass 0.7 kg. Sleep stages carry larger relative error than the global measures, following Svensson et al.

## What this does not establish

The audit shows the simulator is not an algebraic mirror of the engine. It does **not** show that the simulator is a correct model of human physiology. Both are models; only one of them is being tested here.

A successful evaluation in L3 therefore means the engine recovers latent structure from noisy observations under a misspecified model — which is the situation it will face with real data. It does not mean the fatigue index corresponds to a biological quantity. `DATA.md` §22 states that boundary and it holds regardless of how well the engine scores.
