**Layer:** L1 — Simulator
**Date:** 22 August 2026
**Status:** ✅ Passed
**Tests:** 271 total (23 added this stage)

---

## Purpose

Generate sleep, HRV, resting heart rate, temperature, steps and body mass as correlated, imperfect *measurements* of a hidden daily recovery state — not as independent random channels.

That ordering is what makes the engine's recovery model testable. The engine sees five noisy numbers each morning and must infer something about recovery; it never sees the recovery state, and no combination of the five recovers it exactly.

## Validation

Distributions match the specification: sleep 7.5 h mean with 0.84 h SD (§11.2 asks for 0.6–0.9), RMSSD centred on the documented 60 ms baseline, resting HR on 52 bpm, HRV and resting HR anti-correlated at −0.43 — partial, as §18 requires, not a mirror.

The central property is asserted directly and in both directions:

- **A high-load day shifts the log-RMSSD distribution by 0.31 SD.** The effect must be real, or the engine has nothing to learn.
- **40% of high-load days still carry an above-baseline reading.** An engine treating one low night as proof of under-recovery is wrong two times in five. `DATA.md` §11.3 asks for exactly this, and the test fails if the fraction drops below 20% *or* rises above 50%.
- **Deload rebound appears in 78% of runs, not all.** The test requires it above 60% and below 98% — a rebound that never failed would mean the latent stress process was not doing its job.

Measurement error is checked against `RESEARCH.md` §13.3 magnitudes and asserted unbiased. Sleep stages never exceed total sleep. Stress is confirmed absent from every observable field and persistent rather than white noise.

## Findings

### HRV was initially too random to be learnable

The first calibration produced a load effect of **0.04 SD** — statistically present but far too weak for any engine to detect. `DATA.md` §11.3 requires HRV to respond *probabilistically*, and I had over-corrected into "essentially not at all".

Swept the sensitivity coefficient and settled at 0.24, giving 0.31 SD: detectable across dozens of observations, uninformative on any single night. Roughly two in five hard days still carry a normal reading.

The failure mode this guards against is a dataset so noisy that the engine's caution is untested — a model that ignored HRV entirely would have scored just as well.

### HRV responded to absolute load rather than load relative to habitual

Caught by the RMSSD mean drifting to **53 ms against a documented baseline of 60**. Because sustained training pushed the autoregressive deviation permanently negative, a consistently training athlete settled below their own stated baseline.

The fix was a modelling correction, not a recentring. HRV responds to systemic load **relative to habitual load**, because a personal baseline already reflects the athlete's normal training. With that change RMSSD centres at 59.7 ms.

It also fixed a second problem that had not yet been noticed: under the absolute-load formulation a deload could never produce autonomic rebound, because reducing load could only ever reduce the suppression toward zero, never below it. Scenario S3 depends on that rebound.

### A one-seed result briefly looked like a sign error

The first deload check showed HRV *higher* during the block than during the deload — apparently inverted. Averaging over 200 seeds showed the true effect (−0.096 during block, +0.055 during deload) in the expected direction.

The single-seed result was the latent stress process doing precisely what it exists to do: mask the training signal. It is a reminder that this simulator's own properties cannot be verified from one run, and the tests are written to average across seeds accordingly.

### Stale bytecode struck again

A coefficient sweep produced identical results for four different values, because subprocesses imported cached `.pyc` files written within the same second. Same root cause as in L0.2.

Now handled by exporting `PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1` for any sweep that edits and re-imports. Recorded as **D10** because it has now caused two false readings and will otherwise cause a third.

## Decisions

**Load is measured relative to habitual, not absolutely**, for HRV and for recovery propensity. The engine standardises against a rolling personal baseline, so the simulator's truth should be defined the same way — otherwise the two disagree about what "normal" means before any inference begins.

**True recovery propensity spans [0.68, 1.28], wider than the engine's [0.80, 1.15] clip.** A further structural difference: at the extremes the engine cannot represent the athlete's actual recovery quality however well calibrated.

**Scenario hooks are parameters, not separate code paths.** Sleep restriction, illness and travel are driven by `sleep_override_h`, `temperature_offset` and `steps_override` on the ordinary step function. L1.6 composes scenarios from these rather than branching, so scenario days and ordinary days go through identical physics.
