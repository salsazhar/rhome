**Layer:** L3 — Evaluation
**Date:** 23 August 2026
**Status:** ✅ Passed — the engine generalises
**Holdout:** days 520–729. **Opened twice** — see below.

---

## What was done

The v0 engine, frozen after L3.7, run once over 210 days no version of it had seen. Every Layer 3 metric re-computed, with the development figures beside them — because the question is not *does the engine work* but *does it work on data it was not built against*.

The scale alignment is the one fitted on development, passed in unchanged and verified by digest. Fitting a fresh one would have tuned a nuisance parameter on the test set.

### In plain terms

The engine was built and tuned against the first 520 days. These 210 days are ones it has never seen — a different training block, different scenarios, an athlete who has moved on.

**It performs the same.** Its per-set prediction error is 0.0428 against 0.0425 on familiar ground: a difference of three parts in ten thousand. Its per-muscle fatigue tracking is 0.968 against 0.966. Nothing degrades.

That is the result this whole layer existed to produce. A model that fits the data it was built on and falls apart on new data is the commonest failure in this kind of work, and it has not happened here.

---

## What the holdout showed that development could not

**The settled bias, cleanly.** On development the residual bias is +0.0063, which is a warm-up transient of +5.8% and a settled bias of −1.2% averaging out. Every exercise in the holdout is long past its warm-up, so the holdout shows the settled figure alone: **−0.0160**. The engine slightly over-predicts a settled athlete, by about a sixth of the per-set noise.

Residual autocorrelation falls from +0.200 to +0.121 for the same reason, confirming from a third direction that the warm-up was most of it.

**The engine nominally beats a moving average.** On development it lost to mean-of-last-five by 0.0003 — a tie. On the holdout it wins by 0.0020, also a tie on the interval. Two ties in opposite directions is not a victory, but it is better than one loss, and **LIM-5 is updated rather than closed**: the bar for Layer 4 stands.

**The set counter improves too.** Its per-muscle mean rises from +0.520 to +0.592, so the engine's advantage narrows from +0.446 to +0.376. Worth noticing rather than reporting the engine's number alone: the holdout's training is a little more concentrated, which flatters a counter.

---

## What this does not establish

The holdout is **the same simulated athlete, later.** It tests whether the engine generalises across *time and training blocks*. It does not test whether it generalises across *people*, and no number here speaks to that.

LIM-13 stands unchanged: every figure in this project describes one simulated athlete with one training style. External validity is untested and the real cold run at L3.12 is a sample of one.

Nor does the holdout say anything about the real world. Both sides are models; the simulator is not human physiology, and `DATA.md` §22's boundary holds regardless of how well the engine scores here.

## The seal, and the fact that I tripped it

`data/holdout_openings.json` records **two** openings, 56 seconds apart. I ran the tool once to read the result and a second time to capture it to a file.

**The record is not being edited.** The mechanism exists precisely to make a second look impossible to hide, and quietly correcting it to "once" because this particular second look was innocent is the exact behaviour it was built to prevent.

What happened is recoverable and worth stating: both runs used the same frozen engine, the same seed and the same alignment, with no model change between them, so they produced identical output. Nothing was learned in the first that could have shaped the second, which is the thing a holdout protects against.

But the honest position is that the seal now reads *"opened 2 times, first on 2026-08-23. Results from a re-opened holdout are a weaker claim and must be reported as such."* — and so this entry reports it as such. Recorded as **LIM-14**.

`tools/holdout_run.py` now writes its report and prints it in one invocation. A tool that tempts a second run is a defect in the tool.

**The known limitations were written down before the run**, in `LIMITATIONS.md`, so this is a test of a model whose weaknesses were declared rather than a search for an explanation afterwards.
