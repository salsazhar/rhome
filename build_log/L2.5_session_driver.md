**Layer:** L2 — Inference engine
**Date:** 23 August 2026
**Status:** ✅ Passed
**Tests:** 598 total (12 added this stage)

---

## Purpose

Drive the engine's parts through one pass over the calendar in the order the specification requires, and compare its estimates against the simulator's latent truth for the first time.

This stage was inserted ahead of the confidence work at your prompt. L2.4 closed with a note that nothing yet wired the frozen projection into a session loop — the ordering rule was enforced, but the caller still had to follow it. Building the evaluation harness first and letting it double as the driver would have put the ordering discipline inside a scoring script, where it is easy to bend when a number looks disappointing.

## The order, and why it is a module

1. Resolve recovery context on waking, from the systemic load carried **into** the day.
2. Extend the recovery timeline, so the day's multiplier applies from waking.
3. Advance state to waking; declare the day's background activity window.
4. **Freeze a projection for every exercise about to be performed.**
5. Ingest the sets.
6. Score residuals against the sealed projections.
7. Update capacity.

Steps 4 and 6 are separated by the entire session. Guardrail 2 raises if anything between them touches a projection, and a test drives the loop out of order — scoring a session nobody froze — to show the guardrail actually fires rather than merely existing.

> **Corrected at L3.1.** The two fatigue figures above are wrong. They pair
> the engine's day-N state, captured at the close of day N, with the
> simulator's day-N state *on waking* — sixteen hours and one training session
> apart. Paired correctly the numbers are **+0.949** for local fatigue and
> **+0.973** for systemic. The reasoning beneath them, that a modest
> correlation was expected from the misspecification, was a wrong explanation
> of a wrong number.

The local-fatigue and systemic figures are modest by design: the inverse-crime audit records that the engine's single exponential cannot represent the simulator's two-component recovery, and that its linear accumulation cannot produce the simulator's curvature. A correlation near 1 here would be evidence of an inverse crime, not of success. L3.2 sets the standard these have to meet.

## Findings

### The error is the bounded update, not a bug

Capacity error correlates **−0.833** with the exercise's true growth rate. The two worst estimates are the two fastest-growing lifts.

This is the Huber clip doing what scenario S11 requires. Bounding any single set's influence is what stops one anomalous day rewriting the estimate; lagging genuine fast change is the price of that. It is a known trade-off rather than a mystery, and it now has a test that fails if the relationship disappears — because if it ever did, the robustness property would have gone with it. L3.3 quantifies the cost properly and decides whether the rate is set correctly.

### Scoring the best set instead of the first

The first version of step 6 scored each exercise's **best** eligible set against the pre-session projection. Mean residual came out at **+0.058** — the engine apparently under-predicting by six per cent, every session, on every lift.

Nothing was wrong with the projection. A maximum over several noisy draws is high by construction, and comparing it against a single prediction is a positively biased comparison whatever the model does. The projection is a claim about the set the athlete is about to perform, so the first eligible set is what answers it. Mean residual **+0.0124**, sd 0.058.

Worth recording because the bug produced a plausible, uniform, physically interpretable number. Nothing about +5.8% looks like an artefact.

### Capacity was updating once per session

Related, and found in the same pass: the split of `score_and_update` initially left capacity learning from one set per exercise per session. `MODEL.tex` specifies per eligible set. Restoring it took capacity updates from roughly 2 000 to 5 380 over the window, and is a large part of why the estimates track at all.

### A record that claimed nothing happened

`PerformanceObservation` carries `capacity_before`, `capacity_after` and `learning_rate`. Because capacity updates after all scoring, the driver initially filled both capacity fields with the same value and the rate with zero — so every record asserted the session had changed nothing.

The observation record is now written after step 7, when `capacity_after` is true. `learning_rate` comes from a new `CapacityModel.learning_rate_for()` rather than being re-derived, so the trace at L2.7 reports the rate actually applied.

### Mid-session capacity initialisation

The guardrail fired on a case I had not anticipated: an exercise whose capacity crossed its initialisation threshold partway through a session had no frozen projection, because none could have existed at freeze time. The engine cannot distinguish "no projection because there was nothing to predict" from "no projection because nobody froze one" by absence alone.

`_freeze_attempted` now records every session where a freeze was tried. The first case passes; the second still raises. Loosening the guardrail to accept both would have made it useless.

---

## Decisions

**Waking and training happen at fixed hours (07:30 and 17:00) until the log carries timestamps.** The dataset records dates, not clock times. Fixing the hours keeps the recovery window and the freeze point well-defined; real timestamps replace them wherever a log has them.

**Rest days still run the loop.** State advances, recovery context resolves, the timeline extends. A day with no training is not a day with no physiology, and skipping it would break the multiplier's continuity.

**Four exercises were projected but never scored.** Every set of those exercises was ineligible for a performance reading — bodyweight work with no body mass on record, or a set with no effort rating. A frozen projection with nothing to answer it is correct behaviour, not a gap.
