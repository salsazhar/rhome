# L3.5 — Calibration bands

**Layer:** L3 — Evaluation
**Date:** 23 August 2026
**Status:** ✅ Passed — 5/6 in band, one marginal miss reported
**Tests:** 818 total (7 added this stage)

---

## Purpose

`DATA.md` §19.4 sets six bands on how much the engine should suppress projected performance in six named states, each read 24 hours after the most recent session. The section is unusually direct about their status: *acceptance criteria, not descriptive statistics*, and *if the engine's suppression is systematically outside these bands, the λ coefficients are re-derived and `MODEL.tex` is versioned — the bands are not quietly widened to fit.*

---

## The result

| State, 24 h after the last session | Band | Engine | Simulator | Upper-body gap | |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Fresh; after a deload week | 0–1.5% | 1.36% | 1.07% | 0.6pp | ✅ |
| One ordinary hard session | 4–6% | 5.87% | 6.00% | 1.9pp | ✅ |
| One failure-heavy session | 5–7% | **7.51%** | 9.26% | 2.4pp | ❌ +0.51pp |
| Ordinary week, two sessions in 72 h | 6–8% | 7.34% | 8.18% | 2.4pp | ✅ |
| Within a failure-heavy block | 8–10% | 9.37% | 13.16% | 3.0pp | ✅ |
| Peak of a deliberate overload block | 13–16% | 14.97% | 25.67% | 4.9pp | ✅ |

**The numerical-stability clip binds on 0 of 2 087 projections** across the development window. §19.4 requires it not to bind outside S1, S2 and the overload block; it does not bind at all.

### In plain terms

The question each row asks is: *given this much recent training, how much weaker should the model expect you to be tomorrow?*

Take the second row. A hard leg day — squats, Romanian deadlifts, leg presses, leg curls, calves, seventeen working sets — and then a day's rest. The specification says the model should expect you to be **4–6% weaker** than fresh. The engine says **5.87%**, and the simulator, using different maths, says 6.00%. So on the commonest state in training, the engine's answer sits comfortably inside the band and lands within 0.13 percentage points of the simulator's independent one.

The failure row is the miss. Same session taken to failure instead of stopping two reps short: the band says 5–7%, the engine says 7.51%. Half a percentage point too pessimistic, on a band two points wide.

### The one miss, not widened

One band, missed by 0.51pp, is not "systematically outside". §19.4's condition for re-deriving λ is not met, and the band is not being moved to accommodate the engine either. It is recorded as a marginal miss, and a test enforces exactly that reading: at most one case may fall outside, and it must overshoot by less than a full percentage point. If it drifts further, the test fails and λ is re-derived rather than the band relaxed.

## Findings

### Two more measurement errors of my own, both in the same direction

The first run reported **five of six outside the band**, with the engine at roughly 60% of every documented value. That is exactly the shape of result that invites re-deriving λ, and it would have been wrong twice over.

**A session is a training day, not four sets.** I built "one ordinary hard session" as 4×8 back squats. The bands were calibrated on a whole leg day — five exercises, seventeen working sets. §19.4 says "one ordinary hard session *for that muscle*", and an ordinary hard lower-body session is a leg day. Fixing this moved the ordinary case from 2.44% to 3.31%, and brought the upper-body gap from 0.5pp to a plausible 1.1pp.

**The 24 hours was counted twice.** Protocol events are described as ages — hours *before* the reading — so an event at age 24 already *is* the session a day before. I then advanced a further 24 hours past the last event, decaying every state twice. Fixing it moved the ordinary case from 3.31% to 5.87%.

What kept both from being written up as a calibration failure was `tools/calibration_check.py`, the prototype the bands were derived from at L0. Running it produced 5.56%, 6.94%, 7.18%, 8.95%, 15.01% — the documented bands exactly. An engine at 60% of a prototype using identical λ and identical dose scales is not a miscalibrated engine; it is a mismeasured one, and the gap was large enough to say so.

**The general lesson, now three stages old.** L3.3 found three confounded metrics, L3.4 three wrong windows, and L3.5 two wrong protocols. Every one produced a plausible number with an available story. The thing that has caught all eight is checking the instrument against something independent — the specification's own words, or an earlier tool — before accepting what it returned.

### The compression, on a third measure

The simulator suppresses more than the engine everywhere above the fresh state, and the gap widens with accumulation: 6.00% against 5.87% after one session, 25.67% against 14.97% at the overload peak.

This is the same pattern the ranking tests found at L3.4, arriving through a completely different measure. The two suppression forms are structurally different — engine $1-e^{-b}$ with a clip, simulator $b/(1+b)$ unclipped, with different λ behind each — so their agreement in the ordinary range is evidence rather than arithmetic, and their divergence under accumulation is the linear engine failing to keep up with a curved world.

It is the third independent argument for **L5.3**.

---

## Decisions

**Both suppression forms are computed and reported.** §19.4 says the bands are "checked against the simulator's own suppression of expressible performance", and with different functional forms and different λ values on each side, agreement means something. Reporting only the engine would have hidden that the two agree closely on ordinary states and diverge only under heavy accumulation.

**Upper and lower body are both read.** The documented expectation is that upper-body suppression runs about a percentage point below lower-body, and that this is a correct prediction rather than an error. Measured at the ordinary-session case: **1.9pp**. The gap widens at higher volumes, but the sessions are not matched in set count there, so only the ordinary row is a fair reading of the claim.

---

## Deviations from specification

One band missed by 0.51pp, reported above. Not widened, not re-derived.

## Notes carried forward

- The failure-heavy single session is the only miss, and it is the state most sensitive to $\phi_F(0)/\phi_F(2) = 1.35$. `MODEL.tex` notes that this ratio carries the whole failure-versus-ordinary gap and that the evidence constrains the ordering rather than the magnitude. If L5.3's curvature changes the accumulation behaviour, this band should be re-read before anything is adjusted.
- The upper-lower gap at the overload peak is 4.9pp against a documented expectation of roughly 1pp, but the upper and lower protocols differ in set count there. A matched-volume version would be a fairer test and is not built.
