## This stage was skipped and the checkpoint did not catch it

L0.4 was planned as the last stage of Layer 0. It was never built. Checkpoint 0 was recorded as passed with the stage still marked incomplete in the index, and the omission survived the whole of Layer 1.

Two things allowed it. The checkpoint reviewed the artefacts it expected to see — the exercise metadata and the guardrail demonstrations — and never asked whether its own layer's stages were finished. And Layer 1 never needed the module: the simulator correctly uses its own relative-intensity curve rather than the engine's population one, precisely so the engine cannot invert its own generator. The gap surfaced the moment L2.1 first required the engine-side curve, which is the first place it could have.

The process fix is in `tests/test_build_log_integrity.py`: the index is now self-checking. A checkpoint marked passed while any preceding stage is incomplete fails the suite, every completed stage must have a build-log entry, and every entry must have an index row. Recorded as **D14**.

The stage itself is built here, out of order, before anything depends on it.

## Purpose

Turn a logged set into a statement about how heavy it was. Five repetitions with one left in reserve implies roughly a six-repetition maximum load; the population relationship between repetitions-to-failure and percentage of one-repetition maximum turns that into a number.

## Design

**The population curve lives in `rhome_core`; the simulator's does not.** Nuzzo et al. is evidence-backed population data, shared reference material like the muscle taxonomy. The simulator deliberately uses a different functional form with per-athlete deviations, and keeping them apart is what stops the engine inverting its own generator.

**The tabulated relationship is reproduced exactly**, and a test asserts every published value round-trips to itself.

**The domain policy differs by direction**, as `MODEL.tex` specifies. Above 95% of maximum the curve extends linearly to the definitional anchor — one maximal repetition is 100% of a one-repetition maximum by construction — rather than clipping, because clipping would make a true single indistinguishable from a heavy triple and stop the model ever observing a genuine maximum. Below 50% the relationship flattens and between-person variation grows, so the value is clipped and marked low confidence rather than extrapolated.

---

## A gap in our own documents

`MODEL.tex` states that exercise-specific curves should be used for bench press and leg press, and `RESEARCH.md` §5.1 records that Nuzzo et al. provide them. **Neither document reproduces the tables.**

So they could not be implemented faithfully. What the documents do record is the direction — leg press permits more repetitions than bench press across the loading spectrum — and that is what has been implemented, as a multiplicative factor on repetitions achievable at a given intensity.

The consequence is honest but real: these two curves are **Grade C where the general curve is Grade A**, and the module says so. Obtaining the published tables is a documented improvement rather than a change of approach.

---

## Validation

The tabulated values round-trip exactly. Relative intensity is monotone decreasing in repetitions across the whole domain, including both extension regions. Confidence labels match the documented policy at each boundary. One repetition at zero reserve returns exactly 1.00.

Exercise curves are asserted in the direction Nuzzo reports: for the same set, leg press implies a *higher* relative intensity than bench press. That is the correct reading and easy to get backwards — if an athlete can normally perform many repetitions on a movement and managed only six, the load must have been heavy relative to their maximum.

---

## Deviations from specification

The exercise-specific curves approximate a direction rather than reproducing tables our documents do not contain. Recorded above and in the module.
