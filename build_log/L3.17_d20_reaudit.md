# L3.17 — The D20 re-audit: what the engine is worth against a competent competitor

**Date:** 26 August 2026
**Status:** ⛔ the headline results do not survive — 12 tests, whole suite 938 passed
**Modules:** `src/rhome_eval/state_baselines.py`, `state_metrics.py`, `ranking.py`, `calibration.py`
**Tests:** `tests/test_state_attributed_baselines.py`, `tests/test_ranking_calibration_baselines.py`
**Clauses:** `DATA:cross-sectional-muscle-reconstruction`, `DATA:attributed-baseline-required`
**Decision:** D23 · **Limitation:** LIM-40 · **Retracts:** the comparison in L3.2

## The one-sentence result

Given the exercise-to-muscle map and nothing else, a decaying set counter
reconstructs per-muscle latent fatigue at **+0.955** against the engine's
**+0.965**, gets **five of the six** ranking orderings for free, and the
project's most-quoted number — "+0.966 against a set counter's +0.520" — was
measuring the absence of a lookup table.

## Why this was looked at

An external review of the L3.15 diagnostic asked for one thing the diagnostic
did not report: the decaying set counter's *cross-sectional* per-muscle score.
It guessed the answer mattered — "if the trivial counter gets 0.9+, much of the
apparent synthetic success is likely driven by recency plus shared muscle
taxonomy". It does, and it is.

## What the competitor is

One state variable per muscle, one half-life, and a weight column read straight
out of `exercise_library_v1_2.csv`. It has **no** repetitions, effort,
proximity to failure, relative intensity, novelty, wearable signal, recovery
multiplier or within-session amplification. It cannot see a set's contents at
all — only that a set of some exercise happened.

D23 states the principle it embodies: *a trivial competitor is given every
structure the engine is not claiming credit for.* The engine claims to infer
fatigue physiology from a log. It does not claim to have authored the muscle
map. So the map goes to the competitor.

## Latent state

| predictor | longitudinal | cross-sectional | per-day cross-sectional |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| engine | +0.965 | +0.941 | +0.928 |
| **c_F-weighted counter** | **+0.955** | **+0.934** | **+0.929** |
| primary-muscle counter | +0.902 | +0.857 | +0.843 |
| *unattributed counter (the old comparison)* | *+0.520* | *n/a* | *n/a* |

**Advantage: +0.010.** Best anywhere is +0.018 (upper_back, glutes); on abs it
is +0.000. On the per-day measure the counter is marginally ahead.

Not a tuning artefact: the counter holds +0.92 to +0.935 across a 24–48 h
half-life sweep.

The L3.2 entry argued that a counter "looks competent exactly where the athlete
trains everything, and fails where attribution is required", citing anterior
delts at +0.230 and chest at +0.317. Attributed, those become **+0.973** and
**+0.964**. The claimed pattern inverts precisely where the argument rested on
it. Retracted in place in `L3.2_state_reconstruction.md`.

## The cross-sectional metric now exists

The +0.941 figure appeared in the L3.15 diagnostic and three build-log entries
**before any code computed it.** It is now in `state_metrics.py` and in
`DATA.md` §19.1, reported pooled and averaged per day.

It matters because a longitudinal per-muscle correlation is dominated by "was
this athlete training recently", which is common to all sixteen and therefore
free. The between-muscle ordering within a day is what a body map actually
claims, and it is **74%** of the true latent variance.

## Ranking: six correct, one earned

| ordering | engine | counter |
|---|---|---|
| failure vs matched-volume non-failure | correct | **ties** |
| high volume vs ordinary volume | correct | right |
| lower-body compound vs arm isolation, systemic | correct | right |
| heavy compound vs isolation, neuromuscular | correct | right |
| deload week vs accumulation week | correct | right |
| post-deload vs pre-deload performance | correct | right |

**1/6 earned.** The one the engine wins is the volume-matched pair, where a set
counter must tie by construction — which is exactly the case a matched pair was
designed to create, so the design was right and only the reporting was missing.

The deload rebound is free to anything that decays: the counter falls 9.1%
across those boundaries against the engine's projected 2.0% gain.

`ranking.py` and `calibration.py` had **no competitor of any kind** before this
stage, so "all six orderings correct" had run for two stages with nothing
beside it. That is the precise condition D20 exists to prevent, on a claim
quoted in `CLAUDE.md`.

## Calibration: the one place the engine leads

| | in band |
|---|---:|
| simulator | 6/6 |
| **engine** | **2/6** |
| counter (one fitted scale) | 1/6 |

The competitor is allowed a single least-squares scale so it lands in the right
units and the comparison is about structure. The engine leads it, and both
trail the simulator — which D21 already recorded and does not change here.

## A stale figure found on the way

`CLAUDE.md` said the engine "expresses about 67% of the true contrast" and the
L3.15 diagnostic said ~70%. The report prints **80%**. Nothing in this stage
touched the engine margins; the documents had simply not been updated after
L3.14's repair. Corrected.

## What this does not say

It does not say the engine is wrong, and it retracts no measurement — every
engine figure reproduces exactly, including the +0.966 and the +0.941. What is
retracted is the *comparison*, and with it the claim that the per-muscle body
map has been shown to earn its place.

### The last candidate was tested, and it fell too

Neuromuscular peak timing was the claim most expected to survive. The
unattributed counter collapsed there — 81.5% against 44.6% — and the L3.2
argument was that neuromuscular strain depends on relative intensity, which no
set counter has. It was tempting to record that as the engine's surviving
advantage. D19 says a plausible explanation is not a check, so it was measured.

| channel, peak within a day | engine | counter, sets only | counter, authored column |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| local fatigue | **91.5%** | 86.2% | 89.2% |
| neuromuscular | 81.5% | 44.6% | **88.5%** |
| systemic | **81.5%** | 80.0% | 80.0% |

Weighting the counter by compoundness alone takes neuromuscular timing to
86.9%; adding squared relative intensity takes it to **88.5%**, seven points
**above** the engine. The 44.6% was not measuring the difficulty of the
problem, it was measuring a counter nobody had given the compoundness column.

The tie-break in D23 decides whether the intensity curve is the competitor's
to have: the Nuzzo relationship is a published population curve in
`rhome_core`, and turning logged repetitions and RIR into an intensity through
it is a lookup rather than an inference — so the competitor gets it. Even
without it, compoundness alone is enough to beat the engine here.

**So calibration is the only place in this re-audit where the engine leads.**

## Verification

Every new metric had to reproduce a published number before anything was
concluded from it. This is not ceremony: a hand-rolled cross-sectional
calculation during this stage returned **+0.279** where the repo gets +0.941,
because it missed the 1-day pairing offset at `rhome_eval/run.py:49`.
Reproducing the known number is what caught it.

```bash
python3 tools/state_report.py        # +0.965 / +0.941 / 26.0%–74.0% split
python3 tools/ranking_report.py      # 6/6 correct, 1/6 earned
python3 tools/calibration_report.py  # engine 2/6, counter 1/6, simulator 6/6
```

## Consequence

Recorded as [LIM-40]. The closing question for the fixing phase is now sharper
than when it began: if the engine buys +0.010 on latent state and one ordering
in six, then `PRODUCT.md`'s per-muscle body-map claim needs restating, and
Layer 4's plan to personalise muscle-specific half-lives is poor value while
muscle attribution is mostly the authored map doing the work.
