# L3.12 — Real-data cold run

**Layer:** L3 — Evaluation
**Date:** 24 August 2026
**Status:** ⛔ **The engine fails the severe criterion on real data**
**Engine:** frozen at commit `0333d20`, pinned before the seal was opened
**Seal:** one scoring opening, three dry
**Tests:** 886 · conformance 100/100

---

## The result

`DATA.md` §19.2: *a model that fits historical observations but has biased or
poor one-step-ahead residuals fails this criterion.*

It fails.

| matched sample | engine | last observation | |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| all scored sets (n=162) | **0.3148** | **0.1427** | beaten by 2.2× |
| 20+ prior observations (n=21) | **0.3513** | **0.1333** | beaten by 2.6× |

Every baseline beats it. Every margin clears its interval — the settled
comparison is −0.218 ± 0.118. Pooled over all 288 scored sets: MAE 0.2682,
bias **+0.1797**. The engine expects the athlete to lift about 18% less than
they do, systematically.

The same engine on synthetic data scored MAE 0.0397 and tied mean-of-last-five.
**The real-data MAE is roughly eight times larger.**

There is no way to present this as a partial success. Recorded as **LIM-31**.

---

## What the run establishes about the data

614 days, 17 December 2024 to 22 August 2026. **99 sessions** — exactly the
figure the build plan has carried since L1.2. 976 sets kept of 1 115 read, and
the arithmetic reconciles exactly:

```
1115 read = 976 kept + 28 deleted + 110 exercises never performed + 1 without reps
```

54 distinct exercises, 862 sets with an RPE and 114 unrated, 967 working and 9
drop sets. 569 Oura nights, 45 days without one, 241 days with no body mass.

---

## One structural difference, measured rather than supposed

Of the 288 scored sets, **21 — seven per cent — had twenty or more prior
observations** of that exercise. On the synthetic dataset the figure was 1 543
of 2 083, **seventy-four per cent**.

The real log is 99 sessions across 614 days spread over 31 scored exercises:
about nine observations each, a session every six days. The engine's capacity
estimator needs three observations to initialise and roughly twenty to settle,
so it spends almost the entire run inside the warm-up regime that **LIM-4**
already documents as its weakest.

**This is a decomposition, not an excuse, and it is not sufficient.** The engine
loses on the settled subset too, and by a *wider* margin than on the pooled one.
The bias does not decay the way pure warm-up would either — by experience it
runs +0.16 / +0.26 / +0.13 rather than falling monotonically. It does fall by
calendar time (+0.226, +0.183, +0.123, +0.072 across four blocks), which is
consistent with capacity maturing but does not distinguish that from anything
else that changed over twenty months.

---

## What was vindicated

The cold run did not fail everywhere, and recording only the failure would
misrepresent it. **Confidence behaved correctly on a real record** (LIM-32):

- withholding wearables cost **−0.2083** across 83 blank nights, against −0.2819
  on synthetic data. `DATA.md` §19.8 asks that missing data reduce confidence
  rather than fabricate fatigue, and it does;
- **5 of 286** capacity readings inside an exercise's first twenty sessions said
  `high`, against 88.5% before L3.6's maturity component existed. LIM-C3's fix
  holds outside the simulator;
- the binding reason distributed across wearable completeness (389), dose quality
  (180) and baseline maturity (41), rather than collapsing onto one term.

The ingestion path also held: no crash, no unresolved name, no invented field,
across 614 days.

---

## What is deliberately not concluded

Five explanations are available for the residual, and **not one of them has been
checked**:

1. the warm-up regime — measured, insufficient;
2. the athlete's RPE habits differing from the Zourdos conversion, applied to
   862 sets through a population curve;
3. 114 unrated sets imputed at the athlete's own recent reserve (D18);
4. recalled body mass on bodyweight exercises (LIM-30);
5. genuine misspecification of the fatigue-to-performance model on a real person.

**D19 exists for precisely this moment.** A bad number with a ready explanation
is where this project has gone wrong before, and the temptation is strongest
when the number disappoints. Each of the five is testable, and **every test
consumes the holdout further**: a model change made after seeing these results
is exactly what `BUILD_PLAN.md` L3.12 means by spending the resource. The seal
records one scoring opening; a second must be reported as a weaker claim.

---

## What this does and does not overturn

The synthetic results stand as what they always claimed to be: evidence that the
engine recovers known latent structure under noise, which is a question only a
simulator can pose, and it answered that question well. `DATA.md` §22 is
explicit that the synthetic phase is "an engineering and model-identifiability
test, not clinical or physiological validation."

This is the other question, and the answer so far is that **internal coherence
has not transferred**. On the one dataset that could not be tuned against, a
model with sixteen muscle states, a recovery multiplier, a fatigue-to-performance
map and a bounded capacity estimator is beaten more than twofold by *assume the
same as last time*.

That is the finding. It is what the whole sequence was built to be able to
discover, and the discipline held: the export was frozen before the engine was
finished, nothing read it but two bounded extractions, the mapping was reviewed
and frozen before any result existed, the engine was committed before the seal
opened, and the result is recorded whatever it says.

---

## Decisions

**Nothing is fixed in this stage.** Diagnosis is a Layer 4 activity and it
begins by acknowledging that the holdout is now partly spent.

**Checkpoint 3 must report this as the headline**, not as a caveat beneath the
synthetic scorecard.

---

## Notes carried forward

- The 7%-settled figure suggests the *next* validation is worth more than
  re-running this one: the athlete logging continuously with Oura attached
  produces settled observations at a rate this historical export never could.
  `BUILD_PLAN.md` already anticipates that regime change.
- Whether the engine's problem is warm-up, effort inversion, or the model itself
  is unknown. The cheapest discriminator that does **not** touch the holdout is
  the synthetic dataset regenerated at the real log's *cadence* — 99 sessions in
  614 days over 31 exercises — where the truth is known.
- LIM-30's recalled body mass affects `Pull-Up`, `Chest Dip (Weighted)` and
  `Decline Push-Up`; their contribution to the residual has not been separated.
