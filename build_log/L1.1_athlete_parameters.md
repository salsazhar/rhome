**Layer:** L1 — Simulator

## Purpose

Create the specific individual — the hidden constants that make this a
particular athlete rather than an average one.

Everything here is simulator-only ground truth. The engine never sees any of
it, and recovering parts of it is what the engine is later scored on.

The governing rule is `DATA.md` §16.2: the simulator must be structurally
richer than the inference engine. This stage is where that stops being a
statement of intent and becomes a property of the data.

## The three asymmetries

Each is a way the engine can be wrong that it cannot detect from observable
data. Without them the engine would be inverting its own generator.

| Quantity | Engine assumes | Athlete A actually has |
|---|---|---|
| Local fatigue half-life | one value, 30 h, for all muscles | 16 distinct values, 24.4–37.8 h |
| RIR reporting | unbiased | per-exercise bias, −0.64 to +0.91 reps |
| Performance noise | homoskedastic | scales with the inverse of compoundness |

The systemic and neuromuscular half-lives are likewise sampled around the
engine's priors of 30 h and 24 h rather than set equal to them.

Tests assert each property directly, including that **no muscle sits exactly on
the engine's 30-hour prior** — a muscle that happened to land there would be a
free win for the engine and would quietly weaken the evaluation.

## Validation

Determinism under seed, and difference across seeds. The half-life spread is
asserted to exceed 6 h, because a spread too narrow to affect behaviour would
make the whole asymmetry vacuous — the engine's single-value assumption would
cost it nothing and never be tested.

RIR bias is required to run in **both** directions. A bias that was uniformly
positive could be absorbed by a constant correction, so it would not test the
engine's robustness to a genuinely idiosyncratic reporter.

Heteroskedasticity is checked as a correlation between compoundness and noise
scale (required below −0.5, observed strongly negative) rather than as a spot
check, so the property holds across the library rather than at one point.

Twelve seeds are swept for physiological plausibility: no seed may produce an
impossible athlete. Capacity rankings are asserted as a coding-error trap —
deadlift above squat above bench, leg press above squat.

---

## Decisions

**Rejection sampling, not clipping, for the truncated normal.** Clipping a
normal distribution to [24, 42] would pile probability mass onto the
boundaries, giving several muscles a half-life of exactly 24 or 42 hours. That
is not what a truncated normal means, and it would create an artificial cluster
the engine might exploit. A test sweeps 60 athletes and asserts that no
half-life lands exactly on a boundary.

**Per-exercise starting capacities are not registered as specification
parameters.** Four are documented in `DATA.md` §10 and are verified against it.
The remaining 23 are simulator design constants chosen to be plausible for a
trained but non-elite athlete of that strength. Registering them would imply
the documents commit to values they never state. They live in a clearly
labelled module table instead. Recorded as **D8**.

**Unit conventions for starting capacity are stated explicitly.** Dumbbell
exercises are per dumbbell, as they are logged. Bodyweight-involving exercises
are effective system load, `β × body_mass + external`, so a pull-up capacity of
122 kg for an 80 kg athlete means roughly a 42 kg added-load single. Ambiguity
here would be invisible until performance numbers looked wrong much later.

**An exercise without a starting capacity is a hard error.** If the library
gains a movement the athlete has no capacity for, sampling fails loudly rather
than defaulting. A silent default would produce an athlete who is
inexplicably weak at one lift.
