## What this audit found

Three readings did not survive being looked at closely, and two were defects in the checking rather than in the data.

**A test whose message disagreed with its assertion.** `test_sleep_matches_the_documented_distribution` reported "DATA.md §11.2 requires 0.6–0.9 h" while asserting 0.55–1.0. It would have passed on a value the specification forbids. A test that claims to check something it does not is worse than no test, because it stops anyone looking again.

**A measurement taken over the wrong population.** The audit first measured sleep spread across the whole record and found 0.936 h against a documented 0.6–0.9. Ordinary nights measure 0.887 h and sit inside the range; the excess is deliberate sleep-restriction blocks. §11.2 describes ordinary night-to-night variation, so measuring the whole series against it was measuring the scenarios rather than the sleep model.

**A check that could have passed on an empty sample.** The adverse-scenario missingness check reported 0.0%, which turned out to be chance on one seed — across eight seeds it is 6.0%, matching ordinary days. But the check as written would have reported PASS with zero adverse days in scope, which is the same vacuous-pass failure found in Guardrail 3 at L0.2. Checks now declare their sample size and fail when it is empty.

## Marginal passes worth watching

- **Upper/lower parity at exactly 95.0%** of blocks within ±10%, against a 95% threshold. Median 2.2%, worst 13.6%. Sitting on a threshold is not a failure, but it is not comfortable either, and a change that nudges it will fail loudly.
