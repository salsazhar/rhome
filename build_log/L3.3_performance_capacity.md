## The gain schedule, adopted

`MODEL.tex` gains a §Gain schedule and the engine implements it:

$$\alpha_n = \min\left(0.35,\ \alpha \cdot \max\left(1, \frac{1/n}{\alpha}\right)\right)$$

The rate is **scaled, not replaced**. That is the whole of the difference from the version rejected in the drift investigation: expressing the running-mean gain as a multiple of the base rate keeps the ratios between the ordinary, anchor and low-confidence rates, so an anchor test still counts for more at every $n$ the update can encounter.

| | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| MAE, all sets | 0.0460 | **0.0425** |
| MAE, settled | 0.0400 | 0.0397 |
| Bias, first 10 observations | +0.058 | **+0.034** |
| Residual autocorrelation | +0.274 | **+0.200** |
| One bad session displaces a settled estimate | −0.01% | −0.01% |

**No robustness cost**, which is what the earlier attempt could not manage. By the time one anomalous session can reach a settled estimate, $n$ is large and the multiplier is one.

## Findings

### Three of my own metrics were wrong before the engine was

Every one was caught by asking whether the number could be explained by something other than the model.

**Capacity was compared without alignment.** I asserted none was needed because both sides are kilograms. They are kilograms of *different things*: the engine's capacity is whatever makes its projections match observations, the simulator's is a neutral one-repetition capacity. §19.2 says "after scale alignment" and I overrode it.

A single global scale — one parameter, not one per exercise — takes mean absolute error from **10.54% to 4.43%**. What survives is real and has a shape: compounds read high (bench +9.6%, deadlift +6.6%, squat +6.4%), isolations low (db curl −11.8%, lat pulldown −8.5%). A test now requires the post-scale errors to fall either side of zero, since all-one-sign would mean the scale had absorbed them.

**Scenario response was confounded by the time drift.** Bias runs from +0.051 in the first hundred days to −0.014 in the last; the high-volume block sits at days 70–91 and the illness block at 370–375. Compared against a global mean, an episode's "response" was largely its position in the record — S1 high volume read **WRONG WAY** and is correct once compared against ordinary days within 45 days of it.

**The direction test was a bare sign test.** It called four episodes wrong on differences of 0.003 with samples of a dozen sets — noise dressed as failure, the over-claiming counterpart of a vacuous pass. A verdict is now returned only when the difference clears its own interval.

### A test called `_established` that established nothing

`_established()` built **three** observations — the bare minimum to initialise — and its docstring said "past its warm-up". Wrong since L2.4; the gain schedule only made it visible by behaving differently at n=3 than at n=20, as designed.

The S11 requirement — one bad session moves capacity under 1% — had therefore never been tested on a settled estimate. Measured properly:

| Prior observations | Displacement from a 40% miss |
|---:|---:|
| 3 | −3.28% |
| 10 | −1.00% |
| 15 | −0.66% |
| 20+ | −0.60% |

`DATA.md` §19.7 now scopes the requirement to an established estimate and records the profile. An estimate holding three readings has little to defend and a 40% miss is genuinely informative; refusing to move would be the defect. This is a scoping of a requirement that was always about a settled estimate, not a relaxation of one that was being met.

### The engine mistakes illness for training fatigue

The one episode that genuinely resolves the wrong way. During illness the engine **over-predicts suppression**: observed performance runs +0.0254 ± 0.0201 above projection relative to nearby ordinary days.

The mechanism is legible. Illness degrades the autonomic signals, ρ falls, and the engine holds fatigue elevated — while the athlete, who has cut volume, is muscularly fresher than the engine believes. It is reading a wearable deterioration as training fatigue, which is precisely the confusion `RESEARCH.md` §12 warns about when it insists HRV is not a load readout.

n = 22 over five days, so this is a signal rather than a settled result. It should be looked for again at L3.7 and in the athlete's own record.

**The fix is specified and deferred** — `BUILD_PLAN.md` L5.2. Detecting illness would be a diagnosis, which `RESEARCH.md` §17 forbids, and would solve one case out of many that fail identically. The engine already computes the right signal: L2.3's conditional expectation separates the two situations cleanly, because when the wearable is reacting to *training* the unexplained residual falls (high-volume block, 0.83× ordinary) and when it is reacting to something else it rises (illness 1.38×, travel 1.19×). Shrinking ρ toward neutral in proportion to that residual makes no diagnostic claim: the statement is only "this is not explained by your training".

It needs one refinement to be correct rather than merely appealing. Sleep restriction raises the residual as much as illness does (1.38×), and short sleep is a legitimate reason for recovery to slow. The expectation must be conditioned on the observed sleep inputs as well as on training, so that only what *neither* explains drives the shrinkage.

## The other open items, settled

**Residual autocorrelation** was +0.274 at L3.1, unexplained. It is mostly the warm-up: consecutive early observations share a lagging capacity estimate. Restricted to settled observations it is +0.126, and the gain schedule takes the pooled figure to +0.200.

**ρ clipping** costs almost nothing. Residual on clipped days +0.0038 against +0.0067 on unclipped — a difference of −0.0029, well inside noise. The bounds are not costing accuracy and do not need widening.

**Calibration across fatigue bins** is flat: the largest bin mean is +0.0166 and there is no monotone trend in any of the three channels. The fatigue-suppression term λ is not mis-scaled, confirming from a second direction what the drift investigation found.

## Notes carried forward

- The engine ties a five-set moving average on the settled sample. That is a pass, and it is the number to beat: personalisation at Layer 4 has to produce a clear win here or the added parameters are not paying for themselves.
- Capacity correlation is *lower* on the main lifts (deadlift +0.46, bench +0.56) than on accessories (lat pulldown +0.83). Likely a range effect — the main lifts' true capacity moves over a narrower band, so correlation is noisier — but it is not verified, and L3.7 should check it is not something worse.
- The illness finding is the first thing this project has found that looks like a genuine modelling error rather than a measurement artefact. It needs a second look.
