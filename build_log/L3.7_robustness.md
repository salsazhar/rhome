# L3.7 — Robustness

**Layer:** L3 — Evaluation
**Date:** 23 August 2026
**Status:** ✅ Passed — 6/6, and two open questions closed
**Tests:** 842 total (8 added this stage)

---

## Purpose

`DATA.md` §19.8's six requirements, each measured by running the engine over the development window **twice** — once with one thing changed — and comparing. A robustness claim tested on a constructed toy state is a claim about arithmetic. Tested by perturbing a real 520-day record, it is a claim about the engine.

---

## The six

### 1. One bad HRV night does not dominate state

Day 201's heart-rate variability cut by 45%. ρ falls from 1.092 to 0.966.

| | Local fatigue shift |
|---|---:|
| Same day | +3.50% |
| +3 days | +0.77% |
| +14 days | −0.55% |
| +60 days | −0.04% |

One night moves the estimate by three and a half per cent and is gone within a fortnight. `RESEARCH.md` §12 requires exactly this scepticism: 40% of high-load days still carry an above-baseline reading, so an engine that treated one low night as proof of under-recovery would be wrong two times in five.

### 2. One anomalously poor session does not permanently depress capacity

Bench loads cut to 60% for a single session on day 302.

| | Capacity shift |
|---|---:|
| Immediately | −2.51% |
| +7 days | −1.80% |
| +30 days | −1.21% |
| +120 days | −0.12% |

The miss registers — it should — and then it washes out.

### 3. Repeated systematic residuals do move capacity

Every bench set 10% heavier from day 300 onward.

| | Capacity shift |
|---|---:|
| +7 days | +1.01% |
| +30 days | +5.23% |
| +90 days | +8.92% |
| +200 days | +9.96% |

Converging on the true +10%. **Bounded influence is not the same as frozen**, which is the whole point of clipping magnitude while preserving sign.

### 4. Missing wearables cost confidence

Fourteen nights withheld from day 300. Confidence falls by **0.40**. Fatigue moves by −8.2% during the gap and is back within 5% afterwards.

**Worth stating precisely, because the requirement is phrased one way and the risk runs the other.** §19.8 asks that missing data reduce confidence *rather than fabricating fatigue*, and it does not fabricate fatigue — it fabricates a little **freshness**. With no wearable input the multiplier goes neutral, and this athlete's ρ usually sits below one (mean 0.986, on the 0.80 floor 5.4% of days), so neutral is optimistic. A fortnight blind makes the model about 8% more cheerful than it should be, and says so with a 0.40 drop in confidence.

### 5. High step counts are not resistance volume

One day at 25,000 steps, against five hard squat sets, both from a clean state:

| | Walking | Five squat sets | Ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Systemic load | 0.0500 | 0.2871 | **0.17** |
| Quad fatigue | 0.0200 | 0.5349 | **0.04** |

A very long day on your feet costs about a sixth of five hard squats systemically and a twenty-fifth of their quad fatigue. Modest, and not zero — which is right, because walking is not free.

### 6. Upper/lower parity is preserved

Across 18 blocks of 28 days: median ratio **0.686**, spread **0.038**, first-half-to-second-half drift **+0.012**, maximum deviation **0.094**.

## Findings

### Three more errors in the instrument, one of which read as a failure

**A perturbation that did nothing.** The bad-HRV test first targeted day 300 — one of eight nights with no wearable data at all. There was no HRV to halve, the two runs were byte-identical, and the result read as a *perfect* 0.000% shift at every horizon. A perturbation that silently does nothing reports robustness, which is this project's fourth vacuous pass. `run_perturbed` now refuses a perturbation that leaves the input unchanged, and the guard caught a second case immediately: an inserted leg session landing on a rest day, where there was no set to copy.

**A parity metric that could not fail.** The first version scored distance from a ratio of 1.0 with a ±0.5 tolerance. It passed on every block while measuring nothing — the ratio sits near 0.69 throughout and never approaches the bound. It was also asking the wrong question: lower-body muscles carry more fatigue per muscle because compound lifts load several at once, and a ratio of 1.0 would mean the model had *stopped* telling a squat day from a bench day. §19.8 asks that parity be *preserved*, which is a question about stability. Now measured as spread and drift.

**A steps comparison that reported a failure.** Seven days of walking against one inserted session, read as relative shifts on an already-elevated state, gave walking at **5.4× a hard squat session** systemically — a flat contradiction of §19.8. It was the comparison: seven days accumulate while one session decays, and a relative shift depends on what the state already held. Per day, from clean, against the same five sets: 0.17×.

**Now the fifth stage running.** L3.3 found three confounded metrics, L3.4 three wrong windows, L3.5 two wrong protocols, L3.7 three broken perturbations. Eleven instrument errors against — so far — one genuine modelling defect. That ratio is worth stating plainly: **on this project, a surprising result is far more often my measurement than the engine.**

## The two open questions, closed

### The illness finding is real, and the mechanism is now visible

L3.3 found the engine over-predicting suppression during illness by +0.0254 ± 0.0201 over 22 sets, and flagged it as a signal needing a second look. It survives:

| | During illness | Nearby ordinary days |
|---|---:|---:|
| Recovery multiplier ρ | **0.802** | 0.992 |
| Working sets per day | **8.8** | 15.0 |

The engine holds fatigue elevated because the autonomic signals have collapsed. The athlete has cut training volume by 41% and is muscularly *fresher* than the engine believes. Both halves of the mechanism are measurable, and they point in opposite directions.

Travel — the nearest analogue, with ρ at 0.892 — shows **−0.0018 ± 0.0134**, no detectable effect. So this is not a general property of adverse episodes; it is specific to a state where the autonomic signal collapses while training load falls at the same time.

**L5.2 is the right fix and is now well-motivated** rather than plausible.

### The main lifts' low capacity correlation is a range effect

L3.3 noted deadlift at +0.462 and bench at +0.559 against lat pulldown at +0.834, and flagged that it might be something worse than a range effect.

**corr(true capacity range, correlation with truth) = +0.650.** Exercises whose true capacity moved further over the window are easier to track, which is what a range effect looks like. Deadlift's capacity moved 24.3% across the record; weighted dip's moved 45.6%. The correlation is lower where there is less to correlate with.

Not a tracking failure. Closed.

## Notes carried forward

- The fabricated-freshness behaviour in test 4 is a real limitation and is not what §19.8 anticipated. Whether the neutral assumption should instead be *the athlete's own recent mean* ρ is a genuine design question, and it interacts with L5.2 — both are about what to do when the wearable signal is absent or untrustworthy. Worth deciding together rather than separately.
- Every figure here is a development-window figure. The holdout is still sealed.
