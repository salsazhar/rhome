## Purpose

## The five structural differences

Each is a real modelling choice, not added noise, and each gives the engine something its functional form cannot represent at any parameter setting.

1. **Two-component recovery.** Fast metabolic (~7 h) plus slow structural (the athlete's sampled half-life), with damaging work loading the slow component preferentially. The engine fits one exponential to a sum of two.
2. **State-dependent accumulation.** A set landing on fatigued tissue costs more. Twelve sets cost 42% more than twelve times one set.
3. **Volume interacting with proximity to failure.** Required by `DATA.md` §16.2. The failure penalty grows from ×1.67 at two sets to ×2.47 at twelve.
4. **Systemic drag on local recovery.** High systemic load slows local recovery directly, coupling channels the engine treats as independent.
5. **Smooth functional forms.** Continuous functions with different curvature where the engine interpolates lookup tables. They agree near the middle of each range and diverge at the edges.

---

## Validation

Invariants first: fatigue never negative, decay monotone without input, time cannot run backwards, impossible sets rejected.

Then the structural properties, each asserted rather than assumed:

- A single exponential fitted to the recovery curve must leave visible structure. If it fitted well, the two-component model would be recoverable and therefore decorative.
- Twelve sets must cost more than twelve times one set, or accumulation is linear and mirrors the engine.
- The failure penalty must differ between low and high volume, or the terms are separable exactly as the engine assumes.
- Systemic load must measurably slow local recovery.

Also checked: the 48–72 h residual tail `RESEARCH.md` §9.2 requires (55.7% at 24 h, 34.0% at 48 h, 21.2% at 72 h); the `DATA.md` §19.3 channel orderings at the level of true state; that stabilisers accrue fatigue but no adaptation; and that adaptation outlasts fatigue by a wide margin, which is what makes deloads work.

## Findings

### The misspecification is a bias, not noise — and it is worst where training is ordinary

The audit fits the best single exponential to the simulator's recovery curve and finds the error is **systematic**: the engine over-predicts early recovery and under-predicts the tail. The sign pattern is the signature of fitting one curve to a sum of two.

More useful is how the error varies by session:

| Session | Worst single-exponential error |
|---|---:|
| 5 × 5 to failure | 6.6% |
| 4 × 5 at 1 RIR | 10.1% |
| 4 × 8 at 2 RIR | 13.8% |
| 3 × 12 at 3 RIR | 18.1% |

Work taken close to failure loads the slow component preferentially, which makes the total decay *more* like a single exponential. So the engine is least wrong precisely where training is hardest, and most wrong on ordinary moderate work — which is the bulk of any real dataset.

This has a direct consequence for L3: evaluation restricted to hard sessions would flatter the engine. Metrics must be reported across the session-intensity distribution, not pooled.

### A test threshold was wrong, not the model

`test_adaptation_persists_far_longer_than_fatigue` initially required 80% adaptation retained after ten days. The true value is 78.8%, which is exactly `exp(-240/1008)` for a 42-day time constant — the model was right and the round number was arbitrary.

Rewritten to assert the property rather than a threshold: fatigue below 5%, adaptation above 70%, and adaptation retaining at least fifteen times the fraction fatigue does. That tests the separation that makes deloads work, rather than a number chosen by eye.

## Decisions

**The audit is generated, not written.** `tools/inverse_crime_audit.py` measures the running code and emits the document. A hand-written audit would drift from the implementation and be believed anyway, since it is the thing a human reads. Consistent with **D7**.

**Simulator physiology constants stay out of the parameter registry**, per **D8**. The documents describe the engine's model; these are the simulator's own invention.

**Session boundaries affect only the within-session interaction accumulator**, never the state. Nothing is discontinuous when a session begins or ends, which a test asserts.
