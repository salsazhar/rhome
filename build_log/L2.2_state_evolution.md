## Purpose

Carry the four dose channels forward between events, in continuous time, with the recovery multiplier and daily step count reconciled against a clock that never resets.

## Validation

The engine's recovery curve retains 58% of local fatigue at 24 hours, 33% at 48 and 19% at 72. The simulator's true curve for the same athlete is 56 / 34 / 21. The engine lands in the right region without having been told, which is what the whole exercise is for — and the residual gap is the two-component structure it cannot represent, exactly as the inverse-crime audit predicts.

Continuity is tested directly on a per-minute grid across midnight, and separately by straddling a recovery-window boundary: the decay *rate* steps at waking, the state does not.

Two properties are asserted because they are easy to get wrong in the other direction:

**The recovery multiplier scales the rate, not the state.** A good night does not remove fatigue; it makes fatigue drain faster. A test checks that a neutral multiplier — the absence of wearable evidence — behaves exactly like ordinary decay rather than like a penalty.

**Adaptation is not modulated by recovery context.** Sleeping well does not accelerate adaptation, and two engines run at opposite ends of the multiplier's range reach identical adaptive states.

A test also asserts that the engine accumulates **linearly** in set count. That is a misspecification relative to the simulator, which is state-dependent, and pinning it here means the audit's claim is checked from both sides rather than asserted once.

## Findings

### Training happens inside the activity window

The first design applied a whole day's step load across the sixteen-hour waking window, then applied training. Running it over the dataset failed immediately: a session at five in the afternoon sits *inside* a day's walking, and evolving to the end of the window before the session meant the session then had to run time backwards.

The error was assuming two inputs that plainly overlap could be applied one after the other. Activity is now **declared** as a background input rate over a window rather than applied, and evolution splits any interval at the boundaries where a rate changes — the edges of the activity window and each recovery window — integrating the constant-input closed form over each span.

That is what continuous time actually requires, and it also handles orderings nobody has thought of yet, which the sequential version could not.

### A documented gap: the novelty form

`MODEL.tex` fixes the novelty cap at 1.25 and states that its numerical form is an engineering prior — without giving the form. The engine needs one, so the decay constant is the engine's own choice.

It is registered with an explicit `unverifiable_reason` rather than cited to a section that does not contain it. The registry's document check would otherwise have passed it against a section mentioning novelty, which would have made an invention look verified.

### Four test failures, all mine

Three assumed a session's sets land at the same instant, when the helper spaces them through the session. With spacing, the state decays between sets and totals stop being simple multiples — so "ten sets equal ten times one set" was false by 1%, correctly. The helper now takes a spacing argument, and tests comparing raw impulse sums set it to zero.

The fourth compared walking against training after different amounts of decay. Corrected to compare at the same instant, where an extreme 25,000-step day costs under half a four-set squat session systemically and under a tenth of it for the quadriceps.

## Deviations from specification

None. The novelty decay constant fills a gap `MODEL.tex` explicitly leaves open, and is recorded as the engine's choice.
