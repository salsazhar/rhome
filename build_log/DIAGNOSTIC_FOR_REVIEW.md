# Rhome — external diagnostic: why does a fatigue model that reconstructs its simulator fail on a real athlete?

**Prepared:** 26 August 2026
**Purpose:** independent cross-check by researchers and model designers.
**Status of every figure here:** measured, with the command that produced it named. Nothing is recalled or estimated.

---

## 0. The one-paragraph problem

Rhome infers latent training fatigue per muscle from a resistance-training log plus nightly wearable data. Against its own simulator it reconstructs latent local fatigue at **r = +0.965 longitudinally and +0.941 cross-sectionally** (between muscles within a day), beating a decaying set-counter baseline (+0.520). Against a real athlete's 99-session log it fails the specification's severe accuracy criterion, and an attempt to validate the per-muscle fatigue number on real data returned chance. **We then discovered the validation test also returns chance on synthetic data, where the model is known to be nearly correct** — so the instrument is broken and the real-data null is uninformative. What survives is a signal-to-noise measurement showing the fatigue signal is **7.8× weaker relative to observed noise in reality than in the simulator**. Separately, we found a data-provenance confound: the engine's memory of "has this exercise been seen before" starts at the calendar's day 0, not at the athlete's true training history, so **42.4% of real working sets carry a fictitious novelty inflation** meant for a genuine beginner. We want a cross-check on the diagnosis and on the proposed direction.

---

## 1. System overview

One athlete (male, ~62 kg, training ~5×/week, several years' training history predating this log). Two data sources:

- **Training log:** 99 sessions, 976 working sets, 54 distinct exercises, logged over **297 days** (29 Oct 2025 – 22 Aug 2026), from a third-party logger (now replaced by our own).
- **Wearable:** 569 nights of Oura (HRV RMSSD, resting HR, sleep duration/efficiency, temperature deviation, steps), from **17 Dec 2024** — 316 days before training logging began.

> **⚠ Note 0 — the engine's calendar starts 316 days before its athlete does, and that is not free.**
> The two sources are joined into one continuous calendar (a design requirement: an untrained gap is information, not an absence — `DATA.md` §19.5), so the engine processes **614 days** total, the first 316 of them wearable-only. The long silence itself costs nothing: at a ~30 h fatigue half-life everything decays to zero within about two weeks and sits there.
>
> **But the novelty mechanism does not decay away with time — it is keyed to the engine's own memory of an exercise**, which begins at day 0 of whatever calendar it is given. This athlete has years of prior training the log does not capture, so every exercise's first *logged* appearance is scored by the engine as a true first exposure, and inflates local fatigue by up to 25% (`novelty_cap = 1.25`, decaying over `novelty_decay_exposures = 4.0` sets) — appropriate for a genuine beginner, fictitious here.
>
> Measured directly (`tools/novelty_seeding_check.py`), by capturing every `novelty_for` call the engine made, in order:
>
> | | |
> |---|---:|
> | working sets with materially inflated novelty (>1.02) | **414 / 976 = 42.4%** |
> | sets inflated more than 10% | 191 / 976 = 19.6% |
> | sets scored as a true first exposure (novelty = 1.25) | **55** (one per distinct exercise) |
> | mean novelty, first 200 chronological sets vs last 200 | 1.090 vs 1.049 |
> | equivalent extra full-novelty-set dose injected, summed | ≈ 48 sets |
>
> This is a data-provenance artefact, not a defect in the novelty mechanism itself — it would not occur for an athlete whose log began with their first-ever training. It inflates local fatigue on **42% of the real log**, and it sits directly inside the channel under investigation in this document. **Capacity is not affected** — a fresh per-exercise capacity estimate is correct regardless of true history, since the log genuinely carries no capacity evidence before its own start (the already-known, already-mitigated LIM-4). We checked whether `adaptation` was similarly corrupted; it is tracked in state and traces but does not feed into `expected_suppression` at all, so it is cosmetic here.
>
> Not yet fixed. The natural fix is to seed each exercise's exposure count as "familiar" at the start of real-data ingestion, rather than assuming a blank slate — a change to the ingest adapter, not to the model. Recorded as LIM-38 and included here because it is a genuine confound in exactly the data this document analyses, and any independent read of the L3.15 result should know it is present.

The engine is deterministic and pure: `engine(inputs, previous_state, timestamp) -> new_state, outputs`. It is developed against a **synthetic dataset** generated by a separate simulator ("Athlete A", 730 days), with a strict import boundary preventing the engine from importing simulator code. A holdout (days 520–729) was opened and is now spent.

### 1.1 State

Sixteen muscle groups: chest, lats, upper_back, anterior_delts, lateral_delts, posterior_delts, biceps, triceps, forearms, abs, quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves, adductors, spinal_erectors.

```
EngineState:
    local_fatigue: dict[Muscle, float]     # 16 scalars
    neuromuscular: float                    # 1 scalar
    systemic: float                         # 1 scalar
    adaptation: dict[Muscle, float]         # slow, 42-day time constant
```

## 2. The model, exactly as implemented

### 2.1 Dose from one set

```python
rir       = logged RIR, or Zourdos conversion from RPE, or imputed (prior = 2)
intensity = relative_intensity(reps, rir, nuzzo_curve)     # fraction of 1RM

stimulus_factor = φ_S(rir) · s(reps)
fatigue_factor  = 0.06 · φ_F(rir) · f(reps) · novelty

local_fatigue[m] += c_F[m] · fatigue_factor
neuromuscular    += 0.10 · compoundness · intensity² · φ_N(rir)
systemic         += 0.07 · systemic_cost · φ_F(rir) · f(reps)
```

with

```
f(reps) = clip(0.80 + 0.02·reps, 0.86, 1.20)
φ_F     = {RIR 0: 1.35, 1: 1.15, 2: 1.00, 3: 0.90, 4: 0.82, 5: 0.75}   (interpolated)
novelty ∈ [1.00, 1.25]
c_F[m]  = 1.0 primary · 0.5 secondary · 0.2 stabiliser
```

> **⚠ Note 1 — the local-fatigue channel has no load or intensity term.**
> `fatigue_factor` depends only on RIR, reps and novelty. A set of 5 at 100 kg deposits the same local fatigue as a set of 5 at 20 kg at the same RIR. This is a known, deliberately accepted limitation (LIM-10, "the model is load-blind in its dose channels"), but its consequence deserves stating plainly:
>
> This athlete trains at **mean RPE 9.58, sd 0.68** — essentially every set to near-failure. So φ_F is near-constant, f(reps) varies only within [0.88, 0.96] for his rep ranges, and across his entire log `fatigue_factor` spans **0.0607 to 0.0972, a 1.60× range**.
>
> **The local-fatigue channel is therefore, in practice, a decayed muscle-weighted set counter with ±30% modulation.** This is consistent with the decaying set-counter baseline scoring 54.5% on the directional test — the best of any predictor tried — and with it reaching +0.911 on aggregate local fatigue in the synthetic evaluation.

### 2.2 Time evolution

```
F_m(t+Δ) = F_m(t) · exp(−ln2 / h · ρ(t) · Δ)
```

ρ is a recovery multiplier from wearables, clipped to [0.80, 1.15]:

```
ρ = 1 + 0.05·z(HRV) − 0.04·z(RHR) + 0.04·z(sleep) + 0.02·z(efficiency) − 0.03·z(temp)
```

| half-life | value |
|---|---|
| local | **30.0 h, identical for all 16 muscles** |
| neuromuscular | 24.0 h |
| systemic | 30.0 h |

> **⚠ Note 2 — specification/implementation divergence.**
> `MODEL.tex` §576 defines the decay "where **$h_m$ is a muscle-specific half-life**", and its parameter table gives "Local fatigue half-life: 24–42 h, mean 30 h". The implementation reads a single scalar (`state.py:99`) and applies it to every muscle. **The mean of the specified range is used for all sixteen.** A calf and a spinal erector recover identically.
>
> The **simulator**, by contrast, samples per-muscle half-lives — on the seed used, spanning **25.9 h to 37.3 h** — and uses a **two-compartment** model (fast metabolic component at 7.0 h plus a slow component, `slow_fraction_base = 0.52`, shifted by eccentric demand and proximity to failure).
>
> So the generator is a 2-compartment, 16-rate model; the estimator is a 1-compartment, 1-rate model. Recorded as LIM-37.

### 2.3 Performance prediction

```
local_exposure = Σ_m ω_m · F_m           where ω = c_F normalised to unit sum
terms          = { local:         0.065 · local_exposure,
                   neuromuscular: 0.045 · compoundness · N,
                   systemic:      0.030 · G }
suppression    = 1 − exp(−clip(Σ terms, 0, 0.25))
projected      = capacity · (1 − suppression)
```

Projections are frozen before any set of the session is observed (a tested guardrail).

> **⚠ Note 3 — ω is an average, not a sum, and it is diffuse.**
> Measured across the 54 exercises this athlete performs:
>
> | | |
> |---|---:|
> | muscles in the ω vector | median **6**, max 8 |
> | share of ω on the *primary* muscles | median **0.556**, min 0.385 |
> | ω overlap between exercises with **disjoint** primaries | median **0.190** |
>
> A barbell bench press puts **0.385** of its performance weight on chest. A median **44%** of any exercise's exposure comes from muscles it does not target.

### 2.4 Capacity estimation

Robust online update per exercise identity, Huber-clipped (k = 1.345), learning rate 0.2063 (0.1591 for medium-confidence evidence), **capped at 2.1% movement per session** (a product requirement: "one bad session barely moves capacity"). Identity is `exercise_id` plus an equipment context for cable/machine work.

## 3. What works

Synthetic evaluation, development window (days 0–519), against the simulator's true latent state:

| | engine | decaying set counter |
|---|---:|---:|
| per-muscle local fatigue, **longitudinal** (mean over muscles of each muscle's time-series r) | **+0.965** | +0.520 |
| per-muscle local fatigue, **cross-sectional** (between muscles within a day, day means removed) | **+0.941** | — |
| aggregate local fatigue | — | +0.911 |

Variance decomposition of the *true* latent fatigue: **26.0% between-day, 74.0% within-day between-muscle.** So the cross-sectional figure is the majority of the variance, and the engine captures it.

Ranking: all six specified orderings correct, expressing ~70% of the true contrast. Robustness: a single bad session moves capacity by a median 1.56%, worst 2.24%, with 0.06% residue at 90 days.

**On its own simulator, the engine discriminates between muscles well.**

## 4. What fails

### 4.1 Real-data accuracy

One-step-ahead prediction on the 99-session log:

| | MAE (log) |
|---|---:|
| engine | **0.2955** |
| "last observation" baseline | 0.1255 |
| recency baselines | 0.1255 – 0.1486 |

The engine loses to repeating the previous session by 2.4×. A coordinated repair (identity split by equipment context, faster scale-aware learning rate, non-linear within-session fatigue) closed roughly 80% of the gap but still lost.

Under our own rules this is **development data** and validates nothing — the diagnosis and the fixes both saw it.

### 4.2 The fatigue validation attempt, and why it is void

We built a paired within-session directional test: take two exercises loading different primary muscles in the same session; the engine has frozen a predicted suppression for each; does the one it calls more suppressed come in lower against its own rolling-median implied capacity? Pairing within a session cancels sleep, illness and week. Chance is 50%.

| predictor (real log) | | |
|---|---:|---:|
| engine suppression | 71/143 = **49.7%** | p = 1.0000 |
| engine, local term only | 62/143 = 43.4% | p = 0.132 |
| session position | 61/143 = 42.7% | p = 0.094 |
| decaying set count | 78/143 = **54.5%** | p = 0.316 |

Instrument verified: a predictor fed the answer scores 143/143, its inverse 0/143.

**Then we ran the identical test on synthetic data, where the model is known to reconstruct truth at +0.941 cross-sectionally:**

| | n | score | ceiling for a *perfect* model of this effect size |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| synthetic | 4228 | **49.0%** | 54.6% |
| real | 143 | 49.7% | 50.6% |

At n = 4228 the synthetic ceiling of 54.6% sits ~6 SE from chance and would be plainly visible. **The test scores chance on a model we know to be nearly right.** The design is therefore not a valid instrument for this question and the real-data null must not be read as a refutation of the fatigue model.

The ceiling is computed as `P(sign agrees) = ½ + arctan(σ_effect/σ_noise)/π` under observed = effect + independent noise.

## 5. The one finding that survives — and we think it is the important one

Same design, both datasets:

| | sd predicted pair gap | sd observed pair gap | ratio | ceiling |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| synthetic (Athlete A) | 0.0096 | 0.0668 | **14.4%** | 54.6% |
| real (this athlete) | 0.0060 | 0.3243 | **1.8%** | 50.6% |

- Observed performance noise is **4.9× larger** on the real athlete than in the simulator.
- Predicted fatigue contrast is **1.6× smaller**.
- Net: **the fatigue signal is 7.8× weaker relative to observed noise in reality than in the simulator.**

Supporting figures on the real log: median predicted suppression **0.0240**, maximum ever reached **0.0475** (against a `suppression_clip` of 0.25 that is never approached); median predicted within-pair gap **0.0032** against median observed gap **0.179**; only **10 of 143** pairs carry a predicted gap above 1%.

Meanwhile the underlying state is *not* flat — local fatigue spans a median **0.748** across the sixteen muscles at training time (max 1.586). The contrast exists in the state and is compressed away downstream.

## 6. Candidate explanations, with the evidence for and against each

### H1 — The simulator is unrealistically quiet
Real session-to-session performance varies ~5× more than Athlete A's. If the simulator understates day-to-day performance noise, every synthetic evaluation has been run in conditions far more favourable than reality, and criteria calibrated there will not transfer.
- **For:** the 4.9× sd gap is direct.
- **Against:** part of the real "noise" is measurement error the simulator does not attempt to model (equipment-context ambiguity, unlogged machine changes, RIR mis-estimation) rather than physiological variability.
- **Test:** decompose real observed variance into within-context and between-context components.

### H2 — ω is too diffuse to express muscle-specific contrast
With a median 0.556 of performance weight on primary movers, a median 6 muscles per vector, and 0.190 shared weight between "different-muscle" exercises, the weighted average blurs between-muscle differences.
- **For:** measured directly; explains compression between state and prediction.
- **Against:** the same ω is used on synthetic data where cross-sectional r = +0.941, so it does not prevent discrimination *in principle*.

### H3 — One half-life for sixteen muscles
Muscles can differ only by dose received and time elapsed, never by recovery rate. The generator uses 16 rates spanning 25.9–37.3 h and two compartments (7 h fast + slow).
- **For:** a plain specification/implementation divergence; the estimator is structurally simpler than the generator.
- **Against:** the engine still reaches +0.941 cross-sectionally against that richer generator, so the mismatch is evidently survivable on synthetic data.
- **Test:** per-muscle half-lives from literature priors; measure cross-sectional r and real-data MAE.

### H4 — The load-blind dose channel
Local fatigue is effectively a decayed weighted set count. The best-performing predictor on the real directional test *was* a decaying set counter (54.5%).
- **For:** the model has little more information than the trivial baseline, which would explain why it does not beat it.
- **Against:** deliberate — the specification argues fatigue tracks effort and volume more than absolute load.
- **Test:** add a volume-load or relative-intensity term and measure against the frozen baseline.

### H5 — The estimand is wrong for the data available
This athlete has a **median of 4.5 sessions per exercise** (half have fewer than five) but **27–65 sessions per muscle**. Predicting per-exercise load one step ahead is predicting an athlete's own choice, which "last observation" wins by construction. We are considering scoring *change* against a trivial per-exercise reference and letting the model supply only the multiplier.
- **For:** the data density argument is independent of any score.
- **Against:** does not itself fix a fatigue model.

### H6 — Fictitious novelty inflation on 42% of the real log (Note 0)
The engine's memory starts at the calendar's day 0 (Oura's first night), not at the athlete's true training history, so 55 exercises each got a fabricated "never done this before" first exposure, inflating local fatigue by up to 25% and touching 42.4% of all working sets.
- **For:** measured directly and precisely; lands in exactly the channel under investigation.
- **Against:** concentrated early in the log (mean novelty 1.090 in the first 200 sets vs 1.049 in the last 200), so it cannot be the whole explanation for a failure that persists throughout; does not affect capacity or adaptation.
- **Test:** re-run the directional test and the cold-run accuracy comparison with exposure counts seeded as "familiar" from the start of real-data ingestion.

### H7 — n is simply too small
143 pairs; 99 sessions; 976 working sets.
- **For:** real.
- **Against:** the synthetic control had n = 4228 and still failed, so small n is not the whole story.

## 7. What we specifically want checked

1. **Is the ceiling calculation right?** `½ + arctan(σ_e/σ_n)/π` assumes additive independent Gaussian noise. Does a more appropriate treatment change the conclusion that the paired design is underpowered by construction?
2. **Why does the paired test score *below* its ceiling on synthetic data** (49.0% against 54.6%, ~7 SE) when the fatigue state matches truth at +0.941? We suspect the rolling-median reference introduces a confound — possibly that the frozen projection cannot see within-session position while the observation is strongly affected by it. We have not identified the mechanism and would value an independent read.
3. **What is the right validation design for a latent fatigue model against real training data**, given no ground truth? Candidates we are weighing: high-contrast pairs only (a muscle trained <24 h ago against one untrained ≥5 days); next-morning HRV/RHR deviation as an observable proxy; within-session performance decay slope.
4. **Which of H1–H4 would you attack first**, and is there a cheaper discriminating measurement than the ones proposed?
5. **Is a 1-compartment, single-half-life, load-blind local fatigue model defensible at all**, or is the two-compartment structure (fast metabolic / slow structural) load-bearing for real-world use in a way the synthetic evaluation cannot reveal?
6. **Is there an inverse-crime risk we have missed?** The engine and simulator share a taxonomy, an exercise library and a relative-intensity curve. An audit exists, but the +0.941 cross-sectional agreement between a 1-compartment estimator and a 2-compartment generator strikes us as high, and we would like that intuition checked.
7. **How much of the 4.9× real-vs-synthetic noise gap could H6 (Note 0) alone explain?** 42.4% of the real log carries inflated local fatigue from a data-provenance artefact — the engine treating an experienced lifter's first *logged* set on each exercise as their first-ever set. It is concentrated early (mean novelty 1.090 in the first 200 sets vs 1.049 in the last 200) and does not affect capacity. Is it plausible this contributes materially to the accuracy gap, or is its magnitude too small next to the 4.9×/1.6× effects in §5?

## 8. Working rules that constrain any proposed fix

These are project rules, stated so proposals can respect them:

- **D19 — a plausible explanation is not a check.** Prefer metrics with a hard floor (chance, zero, sum-to-one). This document exists because a null was twice over-interpreted.
- **D20 — no score without its trivial competitor.** Every figure carries what a decaying set counter achieved.
- **D22 — the historical real log is spent development data.** It cannot validate anything. Validation is prospective: the athlete began logging through our own app on 25 August 2026, and those sessions are deliberately unread.
- The engine may not import simulator code; projections are frozen before their session; every parameter is registered against the specification.
